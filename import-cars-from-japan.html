Transparent pricing

What the price of an imported car includes

The price of a Japanese import is built from a few clear parts. We show each one in your estimate so you can compare it with a local price before you commit.

FOB is the cost of the car loaded on the ship in Japan. CIF adds marine insurance and freight to your port. Duties and taxes are charged by your own country when the car arrives, and they depend on engine size, age and value.

We quote in US dollars and confirm the final figures after the auction, because the hammer price is only known on auction day.

Interior grades

The interior is graded separately from A (like new) to E (poor). For most buyers, interior A or B is the target.

Damage codes

The car map marks each defect with a letter and a number. A means scratch, U means dent, W means wave or repair mark, S means rust and C means corrosion. The number shows size, where 1 is smallest.

Why Japan

Why buy a used car from Japan?

Japan's strict periodic vehicle inspection, called shaken, makes older cars expensive to keep on the road. Many owners replace their cars while they are still young and in good condition, so auctions are full of well-kept, low-mileage cars.

Every car sold through a major auction is inspected and graded, and the grades use the same system across the country. That gives overseas buyers a reliable way to judge condition from a distance.

What this means for you

Wide choice: tens of thousands of cars pass through Japanese auctions every week, from kei cars to luxury MPVs.

Honest condition: inspection grades and damage maps show you the car's real state before you pay.

Better value: buying at auction removes several layers of dealer margin.

Import FAQ

Questions about importing a car from Japan

Can I import any car from Japan?

Almost any car sold at Japanese auctions can be exported. What limits you is your own country's rules, such as maximum vehicle age, engine size and steering side. We check these before we bid so you never buy a car that cannot be registered.

Do I pay before the auction?

We ask for a deposit before bidding so we can bid on your behalf. If we do not win a car within your limit, the deposit stays with us for the next auction or is returned on request. The balance is paid after we win and before shipping.

How do I know the car is in good condition?

Every auction car has an independent inspection report called the auction sheet. It shows the overall grade, interior grade, mileage and a map of every scratch or dent. We send the original sheet and an English translation with each option.

What is the difference between RoRo and container shipping?

RoRo (roll-on, roll-off) means the car is driven onto a vehicle carrier ship. It is usually the most economical option for single cars. Container shipping puts the car inside a sealed container, which suits ports without RoRo service or buyers shipping extra parts.

Is mileage in Japan reliable?

Auction houses check mileage records and flag cars with suspected mileage problems on the auction sheet. We avoid any car marked with mileage doubts and tell you exactly what the sheet says.

What does CIF price mean?

CIF means Cost, Insurance and Freight. It covers the car, export costs in Japan, marine insurance and shipping to your port. Import duty, taxes and clearance charges in your country are paid on arrival and are shown separately in our estimate.

Can you buy a car that is not at auction?

Yes. If a model is rare at auction, we can also look at stock from dealers in Japan. We send you the same inspection details before you decide.

How long does the whole process take?

Sourcing and buying usually takes 1 to 3 weeks. Export papers take about 1 to 2 weeks. Sea time depends on the route and vessel schedule, and we share tracking after loading.
